# Talent Tree — Agreed Clients & Vacancy Source Map

**Purpose:** Working reference for proactive vacancy monitoring across Talent Tree clients where recruitment agreements / supplier terms are understood to be in place.

**Last researched:** 1 October 2026

## How to use this file

For each client:

1. Confirm the employer identity against the **official company website**.
2. Check the **primary vacancy source(s)** first.
3. Use **secondary sources** to catch syndicated or duplicated adverts.
4. Do not assume a similarly named company is the agreed client where identity is unresolved.
5. For Pepkor and Old Mutual, confirm the **specific employing entity / business unit** because group structures matter.

# VACANCY SOURCE MAP — NON-NEGOTIABLE SEARCH ORDER

This source map must **not** be treated as a flat list.

The search process follows a strict hierarchy designed to maximise commercially actionable vacancies before spending time on high-volume, duplicated or low-signal sources.

## TIER 1 — AGREED TALENT TREE CLIENTS: DIRECT SOURCES FIRST

**Highest priority. Search these before anything else.**

For every agreed client in this document:

1. Search the employer's official careers page / ATS.
2. Search the official company LinkedIn page.
3. Search LinkedIn Jobs using the exact employer name and all known entity / trading-name aliases.
4. Search recent posts by the employer's HR, Talent Acquisition, finance, technology, operations and executive leadership.
5. Search the employer name directly on Google and Google Jobs.
6. Search the exact legal entities, subsidiaries and trading names recorded in the client identity map.
7. Search for vacancies that may be advertised under a parent, subsidiary, division or operating brand.
8. Capture every vacancy that falls within Talent Tree's commercial target market.
9. Confirm whether the vacancy is still active.
10. Identify the likely hiring manager, HR / Talent Acquisition contact and decision-makers.
11. Record the source URL and date observed.

**Reason:** Talent Tree already has a contractual route into these organisations. A live vacancy here is therefore more valuable than a superficially similar vacancy elsewhere.

### Tier 1 rule

A broad-market search must **never replace or precede** a complete direct scan of agreed clients.

Only after Tiers 1 and 2 have been searched should the broader source universe be processed.

### Internal processing order within Tier 3

1. **Critical scraper priority**
2. **High scraper priority**
3. **Medium scraper priority**
4. **Low scraper priority**
5. Any source with an unspecified priority

Within each priority level:

1. Prefer **Evidence A** sources first.
2. Then **Evidence B**.
3. Re-validate **Evidence C** sources before relying on them operationally.
4. Prefer original employer / recruiter sources over aggregators when the same vacancy appears multiple times.
5. Deduplicate aggressively before counting a vacancy as unique.

### Search completeness requirement

A source is considered searched only when one of the following is true:

- its live vacancy/search inventory was reviewed;
- its relevant category/location filters were reviewed;
- a defensible query/search mechanism was run against it;
- the source was verified inactive / unavailable and recorded as such.

Simply opening the home page does **not** count as searching the source.

# SEARCH GOVERNANCE

## Priority logic

The commercial priority is:

**Agreed Client Vacancy > Attributable Agency Vacancy > Direct Employer Vacancy > Recruiter Vacancy with Unknown Employer > Aggregated / Syndicated Vacancy**

This is a prioritisation rule, **not an exclusion rule**. The lower-priority universe must still be searched.

## Vacancy deduplication

Before treating two records as separate vacancies compare:

- employer
- job title
- location
- recruiter reference
- wording fingerprint
- salary
- posting date
- requirements
- source chain

Prefer the earliest identifiable original source as the canonical record.

## Evidence labels

Use:

- **Confirmed** — directly supported by employer / recruiter / official source.
- **Probable** — strong multi-source convergence.
- **Hypothesis** — plausible but insufficiently established.
- **Unknown** — insufficient evidence.

Never convert a hypothesis into a confirmed employer merely to complete the dataset.

## Commercial filtering

Where the broader commerce-search rules apply, focus on roles estimated or stated above **R420,000 per annum** and exclude:

- Sales & Business Development
- Customer / Client Service & Customer Success
- Financial Advisors / Financial Planners
- Administration / Office / Executive Support

For an **agreed client**, however, do not suppress intelligence about a meaningful vacancy merely because it falls outside the general market-scraper taxonomy. Record it separately if it could reasonably produce recruitment work.

# Agency-Advert Employer Attribution Protocol

Where PRN Staffing, Communicate Recruitment or Network Recruitment advertises an unnamed client:

1. Extract every identifying clue from the advert.
2. Search the exact job title + location + distinctive phrases.
3. Search company career pages of Talent Tree agreed clients.
4. Search LinkedIn posts and LinkedIn Jobs.
5. Search other recruitment agencies for duplicated wording.
6. Compare:
   - Industry
   - Location
   - Company scale
   - Ownership type
   - Systems / ERP
   - Qualification requirements
   - Reporting line
   - Team size
   - Revenue / operational scale
   - Product or service clues
7. Assign employer attribution confidence:
   - **Confirmed** — direct evidence identifies employer.
   - **Probable** — multiple independent clues strongly converge.
   - **Hypothesis** — plausible candidate employer but insufficient evidence.
   - **Unknown** — no defensible attribution.
8. Never present a hypothesis as a confirmed employer.

## Important

A vacancy appearing through another recruitment agency is still commercially useful intelligence. It proves that:
- the employer is hiring;
- the employer may already use agencies;
- the requirement may be difficult enough to justify external recruitment;
- Talent Tree may have a contractual route into the employer if the end client is one of its agreed clients.
