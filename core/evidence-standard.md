# Evidence and Uncertainty Standard

Every material conclusion must remain traceable to evidence.

## Required labels

- **CONFIRMED** — directly supported by an employer, recruiter or other authoritative source.
- **PROBABLE** — strong multi-source convergence, but not directly confirmed.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — plausible but insufficiently established.
- **UNKNOWN** — insufficient evidence.

Never convert a hypothesis into a confirmed fact merely to complete a record.

## Provenance

For material claims preserve where available:

- source name;
- source URL;
- source type;
- publication/posting date;
- retrieval/discovery date;
- evidence excerpt or identifying clue;
- claim supported.

## Independence

Copies of the same original advert are not independent corroboration.

Prefer independent evidence such as:

- recruiter advert;
- employer ATS/careers page;
- employer or hiring-manager post;
- corporate information;
- leadership evidence.

## Contradictions

Contradictory evidence must be preserved, not omitted.

When a leading employer hypothesis exists, actively ask what evidence would show it is wrong.


## Candidate-specific non-inference rules

Company-level evidence does not automatically establish individual-level experience.

Examples:

- an employer using SAP does not confirm that a candidate used SAP;
- company turnover does not confirm the candidate's personal financial scope;
- company country/site count does not confirm the candidate managed those countries/sites;
- company headcount does not confirm team size;
- company customers do not confirm the candidate personally managed those customers.

Where only company evidence exists, the person-level claim must remain `HYPOTHESIS` or `UNKNOWN`.

## Independent review

Material records must pass the applicable review gate defined in `core/independent-qa.md`.

The reviewer must attempt to falsify important claims and may downgrade or remove unsupported conclusions.

A commercially attractive conclusion receives no evidence exemption.

## No-padding / no-fabrication rule

Numeric research targets describe desired coverage, not required output counts.

If the evidence-backed market is smaller than the target, return the smaller market and document coverage. Never fabricate, duplicate or weaken evidence standards to complete a list.
